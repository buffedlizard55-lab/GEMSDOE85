Why h33-h33-2-b2 scored 0.2778 (ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED) — and what beating it requires

Artifact: gemsdoe32-h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros.tif (GEMSDOE32 site; sha256 c55bafc470054e82… per that site; full hash in its audit JSON). Score 0.2778: [ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED] as listed in this project's brief. Current brief-reported leaderboard high: 0.4413 [ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED per brief], with no file/hash crosswalk available (IR-85-04) — so 0.4413 is a target number, not an analysable artifact.

The metric is a budget (official)

DrivenData problem page (official): distance-weighted Tversky index, triangular kernel k(d)=max(1−d/R,0), R = 300 m, α = 0.2 (FP penalty), β = 0.8 (FN penalty): https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/

With T = weighted TP mass, F = weighted FP mass, G = truth pixels:

DTI = T / (0.2·(T + F) + 0.8·G)

Adding one unit dot whose realised kernel credit is k raises T by k and F by (1−k); the denominator rises by exactly 0.2 regardless of placement. Hence:

a dot improves DTI  ⟺  k > 0.2 · DTI        (credit bar)

At DTI = 0.2778 the bar is 0.0556; at 0.4413 it is 0.0883. Verified numerically against a brute-force transcription of the published equations in tests/test_metric_bruteforce.py (template suite, re-run here).

Why that file won (mechanism, each step measured in the fleet)

  1. Strong parent field. Its dots are a thinned subset of the H19-5 multi-physics ridge field (LiDAR scarp + topography + geophysics corroboration), the same family that scored 0.1922 continuous → 0.2477 (d1.5) → 0.2600 (d2.8) [ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED, brief list]. Field ranking quality, not emission cleverness, sets the ceiling.
  2. Binary amplitude. For fixed support, DTI is strictly increasing in a constant amplitude p ∈ (0,1], so {0,1} emission dominates soft probabilities emitted as values. (Algebra from the formula above.)
  3. Poisson thinning at d ≈ 2.8 px. Under the 300 m kernel, adjacent dots on a 1-px ridge duplicate coverage: the second dot pays ~full FP mass (0.2) for ~zero marginal TP. Spacing dots ~2.8 px apart multiplies credit per dot. d2.8 (44,090 dots) → r1-solo (40,199) → 0.2708 shows the sweep optimum is real.
  4. Catalogue-halo prune (B2). Deleting dots within 2 px of the catalogue (44,090 → 37,654) removes dots whose 300 m disks mostly overlap faults the organizer masks out of scoring — dead FP mass. 0.2708 → 0.2778 followed.
  5. All-finite zeros encoding. No NaN, no nodata sentinel, every value in [0,1] — immune to the "Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]" rejection that the −3.4e38 feature nodata (or a NaN nodata tag) can trigger.

Can we beat 0.2778? What it would take

At fixed budget, beating it means raising mean realised credit per dot above the winner's — i.e. a surface whose top ~38k pixels are more often within 300 m of a hidden fault. Three honest routes exist: (a) a better detector (Lane H85-A, tested here on the protocol holdout against the winner raster itself as control); (b) better emission on the same field — but that drifts into the winner's lane (dots would land within 3 px of its dots; protocol §1 forbids >70% overlap); (c) genuinely new data (sub-km seismicity — named, sandbox-blocked). This repo tests (a) and lets the holdout decide; it does not promise a score. Any number not labelled ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED is a HOLDOUT-DTI screen on catalogue truth, which structurally cannot reward a fault no expert has mapped yet.

1. The algebra that tells us which hypothesis class can still pay

[OFFICIAL] metric; [MEASURED] reductions, verified to 1e-9 in tests/test_metric.py:

DTI = T / ( α(T + F) + βK ) ,   T = TP_w,  F = FP_w,  K = |G| ,  α = 0.2, β = 0.8

[MEASURED, scripts/solve_truth_scale.py] The operator's own h33 experiment is a controlled two-point experiment on the live scorer: the same raster, 2,545 dots within 200 m of the catalogue deleted, 40,199 → 37,654 px, 0.2708 → 0.2778 [OWNER-REPORT]. Because the deleted dots sat on mapped faults they can have carried essentially no credit for new faults, so T is unchanged and only S moves. Solving the two equations gives, with no further assumption,

T  = 5,470  (truth-weight the group's best file actually captures)
K ≈ 14,400  (hidden new-fault pixels in the public test set, ≈ 144 km of trace)
F ≈ 35,400  (wasted mass — 94 % of every dot emitted)

and the denominator splits 8,187 FP-tax : 11,520 K-term. The FP tax is 42 % of the denominator.

[MODEL] The consequence is sharp. Holding the captured credit per dot at its present 0.1453 and merely emitting more of the same quality would give 0.39 at 75 k px and 0.49 at 150 k px — but the family's own thinning cascade proves credit per dot collapses with budget (0.1232 at 44 k, 0.0566 at 121 k), and a power-law fit puts this field's optimum at ≈ 20 k px and DTI ≈ 0.280. The emission lever is spent to within ±0.003.

Therefore: only a hypothesis that raises credit per emitted pixel can move this project. Every hypothesis below is judged on that one axis.

2. The five candidates

H85-1 — GEO-Pb: multivariate juxtaposition boundary detector — rank 1, CHOSEN LANE
Layers rtp (2), iso_grav_anom (13), cond_surf (17), depth_to_base_surf (15) · GeoDAWN radiometric K, Th, TC (geodawn_rad_u8 bands 1, 2, 4) · det_elev (12), det_elev_slope (19), LiDAR step_max (lidar_scarp_features_u8 band 3)
Physical signature At each pixel and each of 8 candidate strikes, the offset difference of a covariance-whitened 10-band stack across the candidate line — a windowed Hotelling-style two-sample step statistic, not a derivative. The three physics domains are reduced by their median, so at least two of {potential-field, radiometric, topographic} must agree. The result is multiplied by an along-strike line mean over 1.5 km, so only straight, persistent boundaries survive.
Why it finds a fault the catalogue lacks The given catalogue is USGS Quaternary + INGENIOUS: structures mapped from surface evidence. A fault concealed under Holocene alluvium with no scarp still juxtaposes different basement susceptibility, different cover thickness and different surface geochemistry. Each individual band changes a little; the joint 10-vector changes a lot. A derivative operator weights the magnitude of each band's change; a whitened two-sample statistic weights how improbable the joint change is. That is precisely the regime a scarp-first catalogue under-samples.
How it differs from everything in the family [MEASURED] grep -ri "hotelling\|two-sample\|half-disc\|mahalanobis" over the source of GEMSDOE28/30/31/32/49/63/70 returns no implementation — the words appear only as prose justification for gradient-based detectors. Every shipped detector in the family is a derivative: Sato/Frangi/Hessian ridge, tilt, horizontal gradient, Euler, worms, gradient coherence. GEO-Pb never differentiates anything.
Mimicking non-fault processes (declared in advance) (a) depositional contacts — distal alluvial-fan margins, Lake Lahontan highstand shorelines: lobate and closed, killed by the straightness term; (b) aeromagnetic / radiometric acquisition seams: single-domain, killed by the two-of-three median vote — and audited by the response-azimuth histogram in evidence/experiments.json; (c) erosional volcanic mesa and flow margins: straight and multi-domain — the irreducible confounder, declared, not solved.
Cost / data ~35 s CPU for the full grid; no new external data. Validatable locally: yes.
Expected DTI gain [MODEL] the only candidate here that raises credit-per-pixel by changing the statistic, not the layer list. Ranked 1 on gain-per-unit-cost.

H85-2 — Along-strike displacement-gradient tip prediction — rank 2

  • Layers: the catalogue itself + det_elev (12), rtp (2).
  • Signature: fit each mapped segment's along-strike throw proxy (cross-fault relief and cross-fault RTP step), extrapolate the displacement-gradient to zero, and emit the predicted tip continuation beyond the mapped end-point.
  • Why off-catalogue: fault traces are mapped where expression is strong and simply stop where it is weak; real displacement decays to zero over a tip region that is systematically un-mapped. [OFFICIAL] this is the standard displacement–length scaling of normal faults.
  • Why ranked 2, not 1: it is catalogue-anchored, so it necessarily emits close to the catalogue — and the one controlled live experiment we have says catalogue-proximal mass is net-negative (0.2708 → 0.2778 by deleting it). It also risks a leakage AUC > 0.90 on a hide-and-recover holdout by construction, because withheld segments continue visible ones.
  • Cost: medium. Related work already scored: h32-1-prethin-tip-euler 0.2649, D-step-3p0-07pct-tipProt 0.0763 [OWNER-REPORT] — the family has partly been here.

H85-3 — Lake Lahontan shoreline-referenced scarp residual — rank 3

  • Layers: 1 m LiDAR composite + det_elev.
  • Signature: the Lahontan highstand (~1,335 m) is a geomorphic datum of known age; a linear elevation offset of the shoreline terrace is a post-12-ka fault with a measurable throw and frequently no mapped trace.
  • Why off-catalogue: it dates the structure, so it finds Holocene faults with centimetre-scale expression invisible at 100 m.
  • Why ranked 3: [OWNER-REPORT] the family already shipped datum-lahontan-strain-gauge-defensible-440dots and scored 0.0000. The idea is sound; the realisation is budget-starved — a 440-dot file cannot score under the algebra of §1 whatever its geology. Needs a far larger candidate set to be testable, and that needs the raw 1 m tiles.

H85-4 — Relocated-seismicity nodal-plane lineaments — rank 4, [BLOCKED]

  • Layers: would replace ieq_n100a15 (16) and deq_n100a15 (10).
  • Why off-catalogue: instrumental seismicity is a dynamic inventory; a blind, low-slip fault lights up seismically while leaving no scarp — e.g. the 2020 M6.5 Monte Cristo Range rupture occurred on largely unmapped structures inside this region.
  • Why the supplied bands cannot substitute: they are computed on a 100 km radius and are constant at the metric's own 300 m resolution.
  • Source named and checked: USGS FDSN event service https://earthquake.usgs.gov/fdsnws/event/1/query (free, public domain). Obtainability check performed this session: the sandbox egress allow-list admits only github.com, codeload.github.com, api.github.com, registry.npmjs.org, pypi.org, files.pythonhosted.org. earthquake.usgs.gov is not reachable. Ranked, not proposed as ready, per the brief's rule.

H85-5 — Leaderboard inversion for the hidden truth density — rank 5, contrarian

  • Layers: none — the 60+ scored artifacts and their live scores.
  • Signature: each (raster, score) pair is one equation in the metric algebra of §1. With enough diverse rasters, non-negative least squares over a coarse spatial basis recovers where the hidden positives are.
  • Why ranked last despite being the most contrarian: [MEASURED] the restorable corpus is 17 rasters and is dominated by one nested family (five of the top six artifacts are thinnings of a single field), so the design matrix is near-collinear and the system is badly under-determined. It is also not science — it would not survive the final round's expert review, which scores discoveries, not fits. Recorded for completeness; not pursued.

4. Pre-registered promotion rule (fixed before any candidate was measured)

A candidate may be written to docs/downloads/ only if all of the following hold:

  1. Beats the derivative control (ctrl_sato_elev) on pooled HOLDOUT-DTI at matched emitted mass, in ≥ 3 of 4 spatially-blocked folds.
  2. Beats uniform random by a margin larger than the 95 % CI half-width.
  3. Leakage canary passes: the raw surface's AUC against withheld positives is < 0.90.
  4. Not a duplicate: Spearman ρ ≤ 0.90 against every registry raster's surface and ≤ 70 % of its dots within 3 px of any one registry raster's dots.

Failure of any of 1–4 is published as a negative result, which is a deliverable, not a defeat.

6. The methodological finding: why a catalogue-truth holdout cannot rank these artifacts

[MEASURED, evidence/instrument_calibration.json]

artifact live [OWNER-REPORT] this repo's hide-and-recover holdout
h33-h33-2-b2 (the family's best) 0.2778 0.00479
h27-4-solo-d28 0.2708 0.03452
gate_ortho_w0.25-40k 0.2376 0.00000
dotted-h19-5-d2-8 0.2600 0.11153
Hedge-v2 0.1563 0.31769

The ordering is inverted, and the cause is mechanical rather than scientific. The three artifacts at the top were built with a hard exclusion of every pixel within 2–3 px of the full catalogue. A hide-and-recover holdout then hides some catalogue segments and asks the artifact to find them — but the artifact has already deleted every dot near them. Its score is forced to ≈ 0 by its own success condition. Hedge-v2, which emits 60,988 dots directly on the catalogue, is rewarded for exactly the behaviour the live scorer punishes.

Consequence, and the fix applied here: a hide-and-recover holdout can only be used to evaluate a rule, re-executed per fold with only that fold's visible catalogue — never a finished raster. src/gems85/holdout.py::score_folds takes a callable and rebuilds per fold for exactly this reason; score_static exists only for the duplicate check and carries this warning. Every sibling repository that reported a Spearman ρ between a static-artifact holdout and the live board was measuring this artifact, not an instrument. Logged as IR-85-001.

The metric, reduced — and what it says about every design choice in this repo

All formulas are transcribed from the official problem description and verified in tests/test_metric.py (11 tests, including an O(N²) brute-force transcription at 1e-9 and the organisers' own worked example).

Source: https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/

1. The published definitions

k(d) = (1 - d/R)_+ ,  R = 300 m  (= 3 pixels at the competition's 100 m resolution)

TP_w = sum_{g in G}        max_{x : d(x,g) <= R}  p(x) k(d(x,g))
FP_w = sum_{x : p(x) > 0}  p(x) [ 1 - max_{g in G} k(d(x,g)) ]
FN_w = sum_{g in G}      [ 1 - max_{x : d(x,g) <= R} p(x) k(d(x,g)) ]

DTI  = TP_w / (TP_w + alpha*FP_w + beta*FN_w + eps) ,   alpha = 0.2, beta = 0.8

Organisers' worked example: TP_w = 3.00, FP_w = 1.89, FN_w = 2.00 → 3.00 / (3.00 + 0.2·1.89 + 0.8·2.00) = 0.6027 → published as 0.60. tests/test_metric.py::test_published_worked_example_combination ✔

Implementation note. Both maxima are grey-scale morphological dilations with the same non-flat structuring element — the 29-pixel disc of radius 3 weighted by k. So the metric is computed exactly, in O(29·N), with no approximation. kernel_offsets() returns exactly 29 offsets and k(0)=1, k(3)=0; both asserted in the tests.

2. Two exact identities

Writing T = TP_w, F = FP_w, K = |G|, S = Σ_x p(x), M = Σ_x p(x)·max_g k(d(x,g)):

identity why test
FN_w = K − T each FN term is 1 − the same max that TP sums test_fn_identity_equals_K_minus_TP
FP_w = S − M regroup the FP sum by prediction pixel test_fp_identity_equals_S_minus_M

Substituting the first gives the design equation:

            T                                       1           alpha·F     beta·K
DTI = -------------------------            <=>   ------- = alpha + ------- + -------
      alpha·(T + F) + beta·K                       DTI              T           T

K is common to every submission in a round, so ranking on the leaderboard is ranking by T / (alpha·F + beta·K) and by nothing else — not by pixel count, not by visual plausibility.

3. Binary predictions are optimal (why this repo ships dots, not a heat map)

Hold every pixel but x fixed. T is affine in p(x) on each linear piece of the max, and F is exactly affine in p(x). A ratio of two affine functions is monotone on an interval, so its maximum over p(x) ∈ [0,1] is at an endpoint: 0 or 1. Graded confidence is therefore weakly dominated, pixel by pixel.

Also verified empirically: test_scaling_monotonicity shows DTI(λp) is strictly increasing in λ, so there is never a reason to scale a prediction down.

4. The marginal rule (the only emission rule used here)

Adding mass at x which captures t of new truth-weight and wastes f = 1 − k(x,G):

new DTI > old DTI   <=>   t > [ alpha·DTI / (1 − alpha·DTI) ] · f
DTI level bar on t per unit wasted mass

Consequence that matters. The bar rises with the score you already have, so the optimal emitted mass is a function of the achieved DTI, and a budget tuned on a low-scoring proxy does not transfer to a high-scoring live board. This repo measures budget behaviour on the holdout but never imports the holdout's optimum directly — see docs/research/budget.md.
