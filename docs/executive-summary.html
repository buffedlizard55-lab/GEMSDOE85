What format validation did—and did not—prove

The writer and independent reread validator check the competition’s mechanical requirements: exactly one band, float32 values, finite values in [0,1], the sample CRS/shape/geotransform, zeros outside the finite footprint, and no positive known-catalogue pixels. The formal TIF SHA-256 is 660b219d247dc44c690a98939bae98c6f9534a144fa2d62acab2eb4bb3096a89.

Those checks do not prove method novelty, private-label performance, or acceptance by DrivenData. The final uniqueness audit is a separate hard gate, and it failed.

Method, mimics, and limits

  • Method: fixed Gaussian gradient-magnitude scales 0.5, 1, 2, and 3 pixels on official band 19; take the scale-persistence maximum and emit separated peaks away from the known catalogue.
  • Non-fault mimics: paleoshoreline benches, terraces, roads, mines, lithologic contacts, and processing seams can produce the same edge.
  • Holdout limitation: known catalogue faults are used as local truth; this cannot establish discovery of organizer-private faults.
  • Data limitation: the feature stack and small grid/catalogue files are hash-pinned owner mirrors, not organizer-authenticated bytes. DrivenData authentication/data access was unavailable in this run.
  • External dependencies: NASA ASF/OPERA deformation, USGS ComCat/3DEP, GeoDAWN, heat flow, and GDR chemistry were documented as possible future inputs but were not used.
