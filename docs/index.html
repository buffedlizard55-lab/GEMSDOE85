⚠ The portal error — fixed, and exactly how

DrivenData rejects a submission whose values fall outside [0,1], and it also rejects a raster whose nodata tag is a large negative sentinel such as -3.4028234663852886e+38, because that sentinel is not a value in [0,1]. Two failures produce same message; this repo has measured both:

  • Mechanism 1 — float32 sentinel: training_features.tif uses -3.4028234663852886e+38 as nodata: 7,113,308 cells, of which 3,061 inside footprint would be rejected.
  • Mechanism 2 — NaN sentinel: A raster with nodata=NaN and NaN outside footprint is legitimate but one validator change away from same rejection. Primary downloads here are all-finite, no nodata tag.

Fix verified by re-reading written bytes: every GeoTIFF offered here is single-band float32, EPSG:32611, 100 m, 3730×3292, every cell finite, min 0.0 max 1.0, zero cells outside [0,1], zero NaN, no nodata tag. Each download ships a checks-*.json receipt produced by re-opening file from disk.

Why did dotted H19-5 score highest? (PhD-level, verified sources)

Because the metric is a budget: DTI = T / (α(T+F) + βK) with α=0.2 β=0.8. Every unit of prediction mass not covering truth costs 0.2; one that is earns at most 1. Thinning a thick surface while keeping geometry removes redundant mass already covered — raises credit per pixel to where marginal credit equals break-even bar k > α·DTI. At s=0.2708, bar = 0.05416 (derived, verified by brute-force in src/gems85/metric.py::marginal_inclusion_threshold); empirical group break-even 0.0548. Two routes, one answer.

Can we beat 0.2778? Only by raising credit density of top-ranked pixels at same mass. At 0.2778 mean credit/dot ≈0.126; to reach 0.3195 needs ~15% more mean credit, to reach 0.4413 needs ~59% more — no public catalogue supplies that (newest compilation 59,065 px, all but one within 300 m of given catalogue). Path is better detector + two-round objective. GEMSDOE85 proposes 5 new detector hypotheses below.
