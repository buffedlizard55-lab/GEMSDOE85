Why we focused on transtensional pull-aparts (H85-1)

Great Basin geothermal systems are not randomly distributed along faults — they cluster at step-overs, relay ramps, and pull-apart basins where oblique extension creates dilational permeability (Faulds et al. 2010 Geothermal favorability in the Great Basin, NBMG Bulletin; Faulds & Hinz 2015). The USGS Quaternary catalogue records the bounding fault trace, but the basin interior has no scarp — so it is systematically absent from the competition labels, yet precisely where a new fault would be verified by the expert panel (Final Prize Round). Geodetic strain (dilatation + shear) images this directly.

Verified sources:

  • Faulds, J.E. et al. — NBMG Great Basin transtension publications.
  • DOE GEMS Prize problem description explicitly lists dilatation rate, shear strain rate, second invariant as provided features — official but under-used (13/19 bands have zero derived channels in prior repo's feature stack).
  • GeoDAWN isostatic gravity basement depth + conductivity image the subsurface contact that pull-aparts offset.

Geothermal vents knowledge (condensed from official verified sources)

Stored in docs/research/geothermal-vents-knowledge.md (see below). Key: hydrothermal outflow requires permeability + heat + fluid. Faults provide permeability via damage zones at tips/step-overs; heat from thinning crust/magma; fluid from meteoric recharge. Favorability = structure × heat flow × hydrology. This repo targets structure via strain & gravity, not temperature (H33 thermal hypotheses already falsified as addition arms, 7-25× too expensive vs τ_live).
