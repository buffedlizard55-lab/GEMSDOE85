## Why the H33 artifact plausibly performed well

The existing byte-audit in `research/why-h33.md` and `evidence/h33_learning_audit.json` describes H33 as a sparse, binary, off-catalogue point pattern related to a broader H19-5 support field, with a near-catalogue pruning rule. This is a plausible fit to the competition's distance-weighted Tversky objective: when nearby predictions already cover the same fault kernel, extra predictions can add false-positive mass with little additional true-positive credit. The asymmetric weights (alpha 0.2, beta 0.8) make missed positives costly, but the denominator still penalizes excess prediction mass; thus tuning point density and removing redundant or mapped-fault-adjacent points can improve the tradeoff.

That is a mechanism-level explanation, not proof that the pruning caused the reported score. Hidden labels, the organizer's score receipt, and a same-raster ablation are absent. The repo's own past records also document that fixed-catalogue holdouts can misrepresent off-catalogue submission fields, so a catalogue holdout cannot be translated into a leaderboard projection. The two different “current high” values in the standing prompt are moving/contradictory snapshots, not a locally verifiable comparator.

## Decision

No experiment is authorized or executable from current evidence. The binding saturation witness says every non-empty candidate exceeds the literal 70% dot-overlap threshold; the project rules explicitly prohibit chance-corrected reinterpretation. The exact transformed cache and explicit method lane are independently missing. An additional provenance irregularity was found: the prior saturation certificate names `data/raw/learning/registry-saturation-witness.tif`, while the learning manifest and `scripts/restore_learning.py` target `data/learning/registry-saturation-witness.tif`; the witness is absent locally. The old certificate remains binding under `AGENTS.md`, but its measurement was not freshly re-opened during this review. The scientifically and operationally correct next step is to preserve this negative preflight, not create a new raster or represent an old one as unique. If governance later changes the registry-overlap policy or supplies a genuinely unsaturated supported domain and lane assignment, H-CHEM-PLUME is the first proposal to preregister; it still must beat a matched, same-evaluator holdout incumbent before any selector considers a real slot.
